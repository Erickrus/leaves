# A Canopy of Falling Leaves

*An artist's statement.*

---

## The Vision

Imagine a street, quiet in the afternoon, and above it — suspended against a blue and clouded sky — three enormous autumn leaves, each as long as a human body, hanging like a dream just out of reach. From beneath them falls a curtain of a thousand thin, translucent ribbons, so fine and so many that they seem to breathe. The wind finds them, and they stir like water, like grass, like a secret being whispered across the city.

This is not a leaf that has fallen. It is a leaf that has chosen to stay — to hang, to catch the sun, and to scatter it back into the air.

---

## The Leaves

Three great leaves drift side by side, each a different memory of autumn:

- The **Ginkgo** — a fan of old gold, the color of honey and fading light.
- The **Maple** — red as a late ember, its edges burning into orange.
- The **Smoketree** — a whisper of purple, tender and a little sad.

And there is a fourth, a leaf no one has ever found in the woods — a silhouette traced by hand, drawn from a shape only its maker knows, wearing cream and flame along its spine.

Each leaf is not a solid thing but an absence — a ghost of a leaf, translucent, so that the sunlight may fall straight through it and down into the ribbons below, as though the leaf were made entirely of the light it lets pass.

They hang at a noble height, all on one level, held up by nothing that can be seen, as if the air itself had decided to keep them.

---

## The Ribbons

From the underside of each leaf falls a dense field of ribbons — glassy and half-invisible, a thousand faint stains of color. They do not fall wildly; they fall in patient order, one beside another, crossing like threads in a loom, so close that together they become the very flesh of the leaf, made of light and air.

They are more air than matter, almost unseen, so that the whole is not a wall but a shimmer — a curtain that one can almost look through, and yet never quite see past.

And each ribbon holds a gradient within itself, a slow bleeding of color that climbs from one hue to the next, top to bottom, the way watercolor seeps down a page. Here and there a ribbon is simply absent — a silence in the pattern, a breath between notes — so that the curtain never becomes a solid thing, but remains always on the edge of dissolving.

---

## The Wind

The wind is not a machine. It is a living thing with moods.

It does not blow all at once, nor in one direction, nor on every leaf alike. Each leaf keeps its own weather, its own breath. There are small, sudden gusts — pockets of energy that rush through a handful of ribbons while their neighbors rest — so the curtain shivers in patches, the way a field of grass moves just before a storm.

At times the wind is gentle, a long slow exhale. At times it swells and surges, twice, four times as strong, because at this height, far above the street, the wind is faster, and it remembers the open sky.

And sometimes, briefly, it gathers itself and sweeps through the whole field, then lets it fall back into stillness. The motion runs at the true tempo of the real wind — the pace a careful eye would call vivid, alive.

---

## The Light

The sun does not sit at noon. It rests low, at three or four in the afternoon, golden and low on the horizon, so its light falls sideways and long.

Soft shafts of light lean down from it, gentle and leaning, and brush the leaves as they pass. The ribbons, glassy and thin, catch this light and return it in small, moving glints — a sparkle here, a glimmer there — so that the whole apparatus seems to be quietly, continuously catching fire, and letting it go.

---

## The Sky

Above it all, a blue sky, deep and clean, crossed by slow clouds, each drifting its own way at its own pace, so the heavens never feel still, and never feel the same twice. They are soft, silent, and unhurried — the quiet witnesses of everything below.

---

## The Visitor

The visitor is a person standing in the street, their eyes at the ordinary height of a human being. They look **up**. The leaves wait high above, higher than instinct, so that the whole work can only be seen by lifting the chin — the way one looks at weather, or at gods.

They may turn slowly, and look around, and the leaves will be waiting. And if they wish, they may change the season itself — and the species of every leaf will change before their eyes, as if autumn were being re-chosen, leaf by leaf.

---

*Made to be looked up at.*
